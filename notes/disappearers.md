## 2026-09-13 · pp. 0–15

We open to the casting call of a gay play. The Jamaican patois begins, and I define terms like "battyboy", "Busha," and "Jamdown" -- but it's not too difficult to guess, if I don't feel like Googling later. This is a VERY modern book. Our narrator uses "mos def" - quite the pivot after reading Bolaño. So far plot-wise, we have a first person narrator who is potentially white-passing (or at least, whiter than the rest of the room) who has responded to an audition notice. All the men are gay, noting that it was obvious who the appeal was to on the flyer. They are going to perform a play about prison inmates, and note that prison is where "the love that dares not speak its name speaks loudly behind bars." I think of the homosexual encounters in the Santa Teresa jail in Part IV of 2666 -- also a traditionally macho society, with a completely different set of norms behind bars.
