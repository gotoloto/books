## 2026-09-12 · pp. 842–880

Yesterday in 2666 was about Hans'/Archimboldi's late-life post the death of his wife Ingeborg, focusing on his one friend, the Baroness Von Zumpe, and later going through a whirlwind summary of his sister Lotte's life including the fate of her son, Klaus, who we met in Part 4 as the arrested suspect of the murders, but without compelling evidence that he was the woman killer. Now Lotte is visiting Santa Teresa with her employed translator. I am still very curious to know if Archimboldi ever sees his sister again or ever truly visits Santa Teresa himself, as was alluded to in Part 1.

## 2026-09-13 · pp. 880–893

We finally see that Archimboldi/Hans, Lotte, Baroness Von Zumpe, and Klaus intercept in a very satisfying ending. Such an intriguing end about legacy, what someone might be remembered for is out of their control - the creator of the Fürst Pückler spumoni-like ice cream was actually a travelogue author. All the more poignant knowing Bolaño wrote this while near death. I teared up when Lotte, her memory failing, asks her brother twice "Will you take care of it all?", a brother she only knew as a child, on whom she pinned so much hope. Now at an age of 80 Archimboldi goes to Santa Teresa to somehow do just that, and we know from Part 1, he was really there. We wonder what taking care of it all means. I don't think Archimboldi and Klaus would exactly get along! But Klaus did have the visions of him being freed by a giant. Maybe Klaus finds his way after meeting his uncle. It's crazy how different Part V is from Part IV which had so much mysticism and almost paranormal stirrings, but it does get linked via the dreams of Lotte and Klaus. 

Overall, this book was a trip -- took me nearly a year to read, with long pauses between the individual "books." Part V was by far the best but I appreciated it so much more thanks to the others. And I love Archimboldi - he's kind of on the spectrum, wants for very little, reminds me of William Stoner.
